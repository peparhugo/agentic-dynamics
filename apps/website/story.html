Origin / first-person record

A $20 API key made a missing measurement impossible to ignore.

I was trying to spend less while I was away. The difference between cost, output, and durable value turned a personal budget experiment into a research question.

At the start of 2026, I was spending close to $700 per month on AI subscriptions. On a train from Rome to Naples, I loaded $20 USD onto the DeepSeek v4 API and pointed my tooling at it.

[H] PERSONAL ORIGIN The $20 key, the train, and the subscription spend are first-person narrative, not telemetry. They belong here because they explain why the question mattered.

The anomaly

Cheap output was not the conclusion.

The early comparison looked like a cost question: how could similar work appear at radically different prices? That framing was inadequate. A passing task does not say what the next agent inherits, how much verification was chosen, whether a defect is caught independently, or what recovery costs after a rejection.

The observation became a more demanding question: how do changing tasks, environments, workflows, and time alter accepted outcomes? That is the field claim, not "one model is cheaper."

A historical snapshot is not a current receipt.

The Story once carried 347 instrumented sessions as a local historical snapshot. It is retained as context only, never merged with the current 1,067-session corpus.

The question scaled

One comparison became an N x M instrument.

Linked sessions were necessary because each outcome becomes the code and context of the next session. Multiple measurement angles were necessary because self-authored tests cannot stand in for verification, recovery, maintainability, cost, or downstream loss.

The result

The field starts where the benchmark ends.

The work now asks what must be observed before a policy can be justified. It has a method, an instrument, a corpus boundary, decisions with authorization limits, and open questions. The personal story is the beginning of that program, not proof of its conclusions.
