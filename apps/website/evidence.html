The Story Evidence

The current instrument follows linked work rather than isolated answers. Its matrix crosses 3 codebases, 2 tiers, 2 codebase qualities, and the existing conditions. The 215 executed stories comprise 150 unique cells plus 65 reruns; their five-session builds produced 1,067 captured sessions across 7 models for a CRUD API, static site generator, or notification service, with every task inheriting the prior codebase. The corpus cost $309.1685 in total ([C], displayed as $309.17). Multiple independent measurements examine verification, review quality, static quality, solution dynamics, recovery, and compounding cost without reducing them to one composite score.

▶ TL;DR — What the evidence shows

Verification is a vendor behavior, not a price point. Across 7 models the test count does not rise with cost. OpenAI's cheapest (Luna, $0.09) and most expensive (Sol, $3.75) models both write ~10 tests per story; Claude's cheapest (Haiku, $1.59) and most expensive (Sonnet 5, $4.58) both write ~120. The 10× gap in verification sits between vendors, not between price tiers. Meanwhile every story's cost roughly doubles across its 5 sessions as the codebase — and the context — compounds. Below are the four facets of the current story pipeline.

Natural Verification Behavior

Seven models built the same story matrix — 3 stories × 2 tiers × 3 perturbation conditions, 5 sessions each, ~30 cells per model. All seven pass their own tests at ~100%. But how many tests they write — and whether they verify edge cases — differs by an order of magnitude, and not along the price axis.

We never instructed the models how many tests to write. Every model was asked to build the same system, but the number of tests and the edge cases covered were left entirely to the model. The difference between Luna at about 7 tests per story and Sonnet at about 122 is therefore natural, emergent behavior, not a failure to follow instructions. The verification gap measures the models' own unguided disposition toward verification, not instruction-following compliance.

The Five-Session Arc

The defining feature of the story instrument is the arc: each session commits onto the last, so the codebase — and the context the next session must read — grows. The Snowball Rule is measurable: session cost roughly doubles from greenfield to cross-cutting (—), and tests accumulate as the surface area to verify expands.

Key insight: the N² term isn't an equation — it's the chained sessions. Every session grows the codebase, and every new line inflates the cost of the next one. This is why per-session cost is the wrong unit: the marginal cost of a change is a function of everything that came before it. A FinOps model that prices session 5 like session 1 systematically under-budgets maintenance work by ~2×.

Grit and Recovery

Controlled degradation made recovery, basin escape, and flailing visible as separate behaviors.

The Grit Spectrum

Grit = Ground-Truth Integrity. How well does a model maintain correctness when given degraded input? We tested 10 perturbation operators across all models, across three perturbation classes: specification corruption (4 operators that degrade information quality), objective mutation (2 operators that change the requested target), and process perturbation (4 operators that alter the search process). Grit is measured as the probability of test-executed success at a given perturbation strength.

Specification Corruption (4)
Degrade information quality while preserving intended ground truth. False premises, removed constraints, phantom successes, contradictory requirements. Simulate realistic incomplete requirements.
Objective Mutation (2)
Change the requested target itself. Inverted constraints, competing goals. Tests whether the model notices the specification changed.
Process Perturbation (4)
Alter the search process. Alien vocabulary, framing shifts, causality reversal, forced abandonment. Tests how the model navigates under unfamiliar constraints.
Detector: Recovery Signals (7)
Markers, tool convergence, qualifier patterns, tech stack convergence, output length ratio, reasoning structure overlap, trajectory distance. Classify every segment as EXPLORATION or RECOVERY.
Detector: Strategy Archetypes (4)
CONSERVATIVE (low explore, high recover), EXPLORATORY (high escape, low recover), WASTEFUL (high explore, low quality), EFFICIENT (high explore, high quality, low cost).

Grit: The Flail Rate

When the model can't figure out the correct requirement, it flails — producing tokens but zero code. This is the flail rate. Measured per-worktree: what fraction of a model's sessions produced tokens that went to narration instead of code? A session is penalized when output tokens exceed code lines by >20x or when zero code files were produced. Current Scope & What's Next

This is v0.5 — an open instrument for Agentic Dynamics. Here's what's validated today and what's on the roadmap.

Covered Today
Python/Flask REST APIs, SQLite-backed CRUD apps, JWT auth flows, rate-limited endpoints, paginated responses, audit-logged systems, API versioning, pytest suites, real-time collaboration frontends. TypeScript/Node.js (initial: 3 variants, 21 cross-model runs). 10 prompt-level perturbation operators (4 manifold, 6 semantic). IEA-baseline EPM projection (1.6%/yr). Cost measurement: tokens, dollars, joules.
Planned (v0.5+)
Rust, Go validation. Structural perturbation operators (broken dependencies, AST corruption, production incident simulation). EPM sensitivity analysis (aggressive/optimistic/volatile scenarios). Human maintenance cost modeling. Pull requests welcome – the instrument was designed for extensibility from day one.
Not in Scope
The instrument does not judge whether narration is wasteful or valuable. It measures the cost. If your team needs auditable reasoning trails for compliance or debugging, the Explanation Tax is insurance – not overhead. The instrument is model-agnostic. DeepSeek was chosen as the anchor case study, not an endorsement.

Reasoning Stability: Cluster Transitions

Semantic clusters per reasoning step — does the model stay in one cluster or hop between approaches? Narrated failure looks like chaotic hopping. But the data says the opposite.

ModelSessionsEntropySwitch RateDominant %Stability Score
Key finding: DeepSeek has nearly 3× the entropy of Claude (0.818 vs 0.291) — it spreads steps evenly across reasoning clusters. But the counterintuitive result: narration_failure sessions show lower entropy (0.42 vs 0.64) and higher dominant ratio (64% vs 50%). Flailing isn't chaotic hopping — it's the model locking into a single cluster and failing to escape. DeepSeek's low stability score (0.209) combined with 89% correctness proves that productive reasoning requires cluster diversity, not lock-in.
Computation: BGE-M3 per-step embeddings clustered via k-means (5 clusters). Entropy = -Σp×log(p) of cluster distribution. Switch rate = cluster changes / total steps. Dominant % = fraction of steps in most common cluster. Stability = dominant_ratio × (1-switch_rate). 5 clusters from 2,215 step embeddings across 8 models.

The step at which a model's reasoning permanently diverges from its baseline. Earlier cascade = harder to recover. The cascade step is the single best predictor of final correctness.

Key finding: Claude diverges earliest (step 1.14 — 86% cascade by step 1) and never recovers (0%). When Claude loses the thread, it's permanent. DeepSeek diverges later (step 1.86 — only 14% early cascade) and self-corrects 86% of the time. ▶ Methodology: Drift Trajectory Classification

Data: 146 sessions with ≥5 reasoning steps, sourced from ChromaDB per-step BGE-M3 embeddings (2215 total step embeddings across 204 sessions). Script: scripts/lab_drift_trajectories.py. Output: experiments/results/lab_drift.json.

Computation: For each session, extract step-0 embedding as the origin. Compute cumulative cosine distance (1 − cosine similarity) from step-0 to each successive step-N. This produces a drift trajectory: [(0, 0.0), (1, d1), (2, d2), ..., (n, dn)].

Classification: Each trajectory is classified by comparing first-half mean drift vs second-half mean drift, linear regression slope, and oscillation amplitude (standard deviation):

  • Convergent: Mean drift shift < −0.04 (shrinking back toward origin) or negative linear slope with terminal drift below 70% of initial jump
  • Divergent: Mean drift shift > 0.06 AND positive linear slope > 0.003 (meaningful growth away from origin)
  • Bounce: Standard deviation > 0.07 with no clear directional trend — high oscillation amplitude
  • Flat: All drift values < 0.1 (never leaves origin neighborhood)

Claude exclusion: Claude Sonnet 5 sessions store at most 4 reasoning steps in ChromaDB (vs DeepSeek's typical 15+). This is not a data gap — it reflects architectural differences in how Claude structures its reasoning output. Within-session drift requires ≥5 steps to classify a trajectory shape.

Cosine distance range: 0.0 (identical) to 1.0 (orthogonal). Typical observed range: 0.14–0.71. Distance metric: 1 − dot(A,B) / (||A|| · ||B||). Embedding model: BGE-M3 via Ollama, 1024-dimensional vectors.

▶ Validation: Independent Methods

Three independent methods corroborate the primary measurement apparatus. These analyses were run on the same experiment session transcripts using orthogonal approaches — embeddings, graph topology, and per-step divergence tracking. Their convergence with the primary metrics (escape rate, strategy archetype, grit scores) strengthens the instrument's validity.

Semantic Clusters
Embedding-based analysis of session transcripts reveals distinct reasoning clusters by model architecture. Models within the same architecture family (GPT variants) show the closest semantic alignment. DeepSeek's MoE architecture produces structurally distinct reasoning patterns — its nearest cross-family centroid distances are to GPT-5.6 (0.0513) and GPT-5.6-fast (0.0547). Perturbation class (semantic vs manifold) produces measurable cluster shift.
Cross-Model Reasoning Distance
Pairwise centroid distance across 8 models shows model family influences trajectory structure more than scale. GPT-5.6 and GPT-5.6-fast are closest of any pair (centroid distance 0.0062), followed by GPT-5-mini and GPT-5-nano (0.0092). Cross-family distances are 5–12x larger: Claude-to-any-GPT averages ~0.068. DeepSeek's centroid sits at intermediate distances to all other models — distinct from both families. These are observed behavioral patterns, not direct measurements of model internals.
Per-Step Reasoning Divergence
Matching-step comparison (step N vs step N across sessions) shows perturbation produces measurable divergence from baseline reasoning. Cross-class comparisons (mixed semantic/manifold pairs) yield the highest mean distance (0.203). Manifold-only pairs (0.181) and semantic-only pairs (0.191) show tighter clustering within their own class — suggesting that while perturbations shift reasoning, models respond differently depending on perturbation type rather than uniformly.
Basin Topology (Neo4j)
Graph-based attractor basin analysis using Neo4j classifies sessions into distinct topological profiles (wide_shallow, wide_moderate, collapsed, unstable). Of 227 historical runs, 203 were classified into strategy archetypes that aligned with the primary measurement framework. Each model shows a characteristic basin volume under semantic perturbation: GPT-5.6 (1.193, largest), GPT-5-mini (0.991), DeepSeek (0.691), GPT-5 (0.618), Claude Sonnet 5 (0.530), GPT-5.6-fast (0.420), GPT-5.5 (0.360, collapsed), GPT-5-nano (0.234, unstable). The validation check confirmed zero classification differences — the scoring is not an artifact of the algorithm.

These analyses use BGE-M3 embeddings via Ollama, ChromaDB vector storage, and Neo4j graph database. Full methodology and results are in experiments/results/lab_*.json. They serve as independent validation of the primary measurement stack — not replacements for it.

The Field Now Visible

The archived precursor and the current story corpus expose part of a shared field without becoming one dataset. Agentic Dynamics studies how AI agents behave, adapt, interact, recover, and produce outcomes across changing tasks, environments, workflows, and time. Here, observed evidence keeps cost, verification, static and reviewed quality, recovery, and long-horizon compounding separate while identifying signals that may support future control rules.

Observed now: controlled perturbation response, natural verification behavior, independent review, static quality, solution dynamics, and the five-session cost arc. Still open: when to route, retry, escalate, or stop; which confidence signals are reliable enough to drive those decisions; and how short-run savings translate into long-horizon value. Those are control questions for future experiments, not conclusions added to these corpora.
