# cap_terra_postmortem — UI/UX decision audit

**Status:** accepted · **Campaign:** `cap_terra_postmortem` · **Subject:** gpt-5.6-terra, both site revamps
**Operator verdict (data):** "complete and utter trash" — the go-to UI/UX model produced worse work than the site it replaced.
**Written:** 2026-08-27. Every terra claim is quoted from the transcripts (`/tmp/wt_site_revamp*/.instrument/session.jsonl`, opencode DB) or its own research doc (`cap_site_revamp_research.md`). Independent assessments are marked as reviewer judgment.

**Baseline that was made worse:** the original site (built by earlier `[workflow]` commits, `Experiment Runner`) shipped a **working interactive layer**: a Framework ROI calculator with **14 native range sliders** (team size, sessions/engineer/day, velocity, working days, energy scenario, engineer cost, EPM annual rate, AI budget cap, workload, batch fraction, retry rate, escalation tier, per-session marginal cost `r_ac0`, architecture annual rate `r_arate`) driving live outputs, a **Chart.js cost curve** (`costChart`), a lever console with two operating lanes, and **five Chart.js canvases on Evidence** (`snowballChart`, `gritMatrixChart`, `narrationChart`, `costBarChart`, `locVsCostChart`). After both terra revamps: **zero** range inputs, **zero** `<canvas>`, **zero** Chart.js on any public page — only static editorial blocks plus, in revamp2 attempt B, one restored beta slider.

## 2. The three special-attention questions

### (a) The interactive layer — did terra *decide* to drop it, or *never consider* it?

**It never considered it.** A full-text search of terra's own reasoning (`TEXT:`/`REASON:` lines across both runs) for `chart | lever | calculator | snowball | grit | interactive layer` returns **no deliberative mention** — the only hits are the user GOAL prompts and the research doc it wrote. The levers, the ROI calculator, and the five Evidence canvases vanished without one sentence of rationale. The closest terra ever came to a decision is the research doc's ledger row ("retire the sales-like calculator… preserve any transparent model as a labeled `[C]` explorable") — which it then **did not implement** in revamp1 (no control at all) and only partially in revamp2 (one beta slider). Dropping the interactive layer was an *omission by default*, executed as "static editorial," not a reasoned design choice.

### (b) The figure approach — why CSS/editorial blocks instead of diagrams?

Terra's stated reason is the research doc §3.3 architecture decision: hand-rolled inline SVG because the corpus is small and "layout, labels, arrows, accessible `<title>/<desc>`, and print output matter more than a chart API," explicitly rejecting Chart.js. That is a *reasonable* rationale for conceptual figures. But the evidence shows two failures around it: (1) the SVG was injected at runtime so the served/static page had **zero** `<svg>` (the exact "trash" observation), and (2) it replaced **real data charts** (snowball, grit matrix, LOC-vs-cost, cost bar — evidence visualizations) with static editorial figures, which was never part of the §3.3 rationale. Good architecture decision, catastrophically incomplete delivery.

### (c) The "editorial" framing — did anti-SaaS/provenance read as "static and safe"?

**Yes.** The transcript makes it explicit: f2's own words are *"a small **static**-publication system"* and the Framework page note *"Those were retired under the anti-SaaS editorial rule."* Terra operationalized anti-SaaS as *remove everything that looks like it computes anything*, and provenance as *label everything*, converging on a static, safe, prose-forward publication. A range input that redraws a labeled `[C]` curve is neither SaaS nor unprovenanced — but terra read the constraint's strictest form and shipped the safest possible object. The process gave terra a constraint ("anti-SaaS") and terra chose the interpretation that required the least risk and the least work: delete.

## 3. Cross-reference to the regression campaign

`cap_site_regression_analysis` (running in parallel) independently formed the **same leading hypothesis** from the operator's truth statement:
> *"the original site's interactive layer (levers, calculator, charts in app.js) was replaced by static editorial figures… (a) dropped the interactive layer for static editorial content."*

This audit's transcript evidence **confirms** (a) and adds the mechanism: it was not a decision — the interactive layer was never deliberated (D1), and the anti-SaaS constraint was read as a mandate for static content (D3). The regression campaign's alternative hypothesis (*"terra's output was never going to beat the original"*) is also supported in part by D2/D4/D5: even where terra chose well (D6/D9), the delivered figure layer was invisible without JS and the palette/type drifted generic.

## 4. Conclusion — where responsibility lands

1. **Process/interaction (primary):** the machine handed terra an anti-SaaS constraint and a provenance grammar but **no gate on preserving the interactive layer or on the deliverable being visible** — the exact gap `cap_site_revamp_followup.md` diagnosed ("the gate must test the deliverable's actual existence, not its description"). Terra's "static and safe" reading was the rational response to a process that never said "keep the calculators and charts."
2. **Model (contributory, real):** a competent UI/UX reviewer does not need to be told that deleting five data visualizations is bad. Terra *chose* the cheapest reading, self-reviewed it as "deliberate and editorial," and shipped a site with zero visible diagrams in static form. The model's judgment was poor even within a forgiving process.
3. **Interaction (the sharpest signal):** the "VISUAL QUALITY: PASS" on work the operator calls trash means **terra and the operator do not share a definition of good design** — and the process gave terra no access to the operator's definition. The fix is not "tell terra to be more creative"; it is to put the interactive baseline under a preservation gate and to put the operator's eye in the loop.

## 5. Ranked process changes (UI/UX-specific, complementing the timeline's list)

1. **Preserve-the-baseline gate:** diff the interactive layer (range inputs, `<canvas>`, Chart.js, calculator functions) between base and result; the phase **fails** if interactive surfaces shrink without an operator-approved reason. Directly prevents D1.
2. **Render-gate the deliverable:** the DOM gate must run against the *served* static page (or a headless render) and assert `<svg>`/`<canvas>`/input counts are non-zero on the pages that promise them — preventing the "wired in JS, invisible in static" failure (D2).
3. **Anti-SaaS must be scoped in the spec, not inferred:** the constraint should name *what* is banned (pricing, tiers, CTAs, conversion offers) and *what* is explicitly preserved (research data visualizations, `[C]` explorables with controls). Terra read "anti-SaaS" as "no interactivity" (D3); the spec must close that ambiguity.
4. **Human-in-the-loop screenshot review before p5 authorizes:** the operator's definition of "impressive" is not recoverable from a rubric — add a required screenshot + operator sign-off on visual quality (the self-review D10 proved it cannot self-score).
5. **Interactive-layer inventory:** treat each interactive element (slider, canvas, lever, control) as an inventory item with `status: preserved | replaced | retired(reason)` — the same checklist discipline that finally fixed the diagram inventory in revamp2, applied to interactivity.
