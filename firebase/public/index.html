The missing variable

Real specifications are not benchmarks.

Requirements are incomplete. Constraints conflict. Context is stale. Previous steps are reported as successful when they aren't. Most coding-agent benchmarks ask: can the model solve a clean task? This instrument asks: what happens when the input itself degrades?

Specification corruption

  • Inject false premises into requirements
  • Remove critical constraints silently
  • Insert phantom "successful" intermediate results
  • Add contradictory requirements

Objective mutation

  • Invert constraints ("fast" → "slow")
  • Insert competing goals that can't both be satisfied

Process perturbation

  • Inject alien vocabulary from unrelated domains
  • Shift the framing stance (construction → falsification)
  • Reverse causality (solution before problem)
  • Force abandonment of initial approaches

10 deterministic perturbation operators. Each controlled by a strength parameter (0.0–1.0). Not prompt engineering tricks — experimental interventions.

What the corpus shows

Three findings from 249 instrumented sessions.

Finding 1

Per-token price does not tell you task economics.

Models produce similar generated token volumes yet bill at ratios that have nothing to do with token count. Token pricing, cache write policies, and provider architecture stack into a 69× per-session gap on comparable output. The measured cost is a function of provider economics, not model capability.

Finding 2

Agent reliability changes differently as specification quality degrades.

When specifications are corrupted — missing constraints, false premises, contradictory requirements — models don't just get "worse." They exhibit materially different recovery behavior, output patterns, and cost trajectories. Some explore novel solutions. Some narrate their way back to familiar territory at billable token rates.

Finding 3

Recovery itself has a cost signature.

When a model succeeds under perturbation, the overhead is measurable — both as extra tokens (Explanation Tax) and as retry/narration cost. Recovery premium varies by model family and perturbation class. Understanding this premium is the difference between pricing a prompt and pricing an outcome.

The concept

Grit = Ground-Truth Integrity.

Grit measures how well verified task success is preserved as specification quality degrades. Defined operationally:

Grit(s) = P(verified success | perturbation strength =s)
Retention: R(s) = Grit(s) / Grit(0)
Recovery premium: ΔC = C(successful perturbed) / C(successful baseline)

This is not "latent reasoning topology." It is an empirically measurable quantity: the probability of verified success under controlled degradation. Escape (structural divergence from baseline) is an explanatory behavioral measure — it may or may not correlate with robustness.

Why this matters

Task economics, not vendor preference.

Once you can measure verified outcome × cost × specification quality, model routing can be based on task economics rather than vendor preference. This connects naturally to established FinOps use-case economics — total cost of achieving a specific business outcome per unit — without claiming to have invented that concept. The contribution is the degraded specification dimension.
