What This Changes

Implications for AI engineering economics.

Pricing

Constraint recovery cost is a pricing-model artifact, not an engineering one.

Both models produce identical constraint implementations in code. The 32× cost gap is driven entirely by per-token pricing ($0.022 vs $10-50/Mtok) × communication strategy (silent vs explicit). This means the cost of "AI resilience" is a function of who you buy tokens from, not which model you use.

Architecture

MoE sparsity is a thermodynamic advantage that pricing amplifies.

DeepSeek's 37B active parameters (5.5% of 671B) consume less energy per token. At $0.022/Mtok, the already-efficient architecture gets cheaper pricing. Claude's dense architecture (all params active) consumes more energy at $10-50/Mtok — the pricing model amplifies rather than mitigates the architectural energy gap.

Strategy

The training regime determines whether you pay for resilience.

GRPO-trained policies implement constraints silently — recovery is free. Imitation-trained policies explain recovery — explanation costs money. For production AI agents operating under perturbation (missing constraints, degraded inputs, adversarial prompts), the training regime directly determines the cost of staying correct. This is not a benchmark finding — it is an engineering cost-of-resilience finding.
