The Five Rules

Here is how you govern
AI costs.

Each rule names a concept we measured. Each concept maps to a lever you control. Start here. Refer back. Share with your team.

1
The Silent Inference Rule. Default to the cheapest model that passes your test suite. If a model produces correct code without narration, route to it. Defaulting to silent models preserves your budget runway for the tasks that actually need explicit reasoning – database migrations, core architecture changes, security audits.Concept: Silent Inference Principle · Lever: C₀
2
The Explanation Tax Rule. Narration has a measurable cost. Models with ε > 50% spend 24–120% more per session on output tokens. Whether that cost is worth paying depends on your verification architecture. If automated tests validate output, route to silent models. If you lack test coverage or need auditable reasoning, the Explanation Tax is insurance.Concept: Explanation Tax · Lever: ε
3
The N² Tax Rule. Model cumulative cost before architectural commitments. Context inflation compounds quadratically: C(N,v) = C₀ × EPM(N) × [N + β·v·N(N−1)/2]. A 10% β error produces a 50% overrun after 100 sessions.Concept: N² Tax · Lever: β, v
4
The EPM Horizon Rule. Energy costs are the inflation rate of AI compute. Local energy markets – not global averages – determine your long-term prices. A team in Texas (ERCOT grid) faces different EPM growth than one in Frankfurt (ENTSO-E) or Singapore. Find the year your geographic energy costs flip your model selection. Hedge before that point. EPM(t) = 1.00 + r(t−2024) where r depends on your region.Concept: EPM Horizon · Lever: EPM(t)
5
The Cost-Per-Pass Rule. Price the outcome, not the prompt. A model at half the cost but half the pass rate costs the same per working outcome – with double the latency and retry overhead.Concept: Cost-Per-Pass · Lever: P
Who this is for: Engineering teams of 5+ running Python/Flask workloads with annual AI spend exceeding $100K. Validated on REST APIs, CRUD applications, and real-time collaboration frontends. If your domain differs (TypeScript, Rust, data pipelines, image generation), use the methodology as a guide and validate with your own data.
The parameters you control
The Levers

What you control. What compounds.
What you must model.

The equation takes eight inputs. They fall into three categories. Understanding which is which means understanding which decisions are safe and which commit you for years.

C₀
Which model you call. $0.006 to $0.537 per session. Your primary cost lever. Two-way door – switch per task type.
P
Whether it produces working code. DeepSeek: 100% Flask-pinned, 72% general with correct env. Pin frameworks to maximize this.
ε
How much it narrates. Explanation Tax. 0% (Claude bills it all as output) to 101% (nano generates more reasoning than code). Pay only when you need auditable reasoning.
β
How fast your codebase grows. Context inflation per line generated. Compounds quadratically. A 10% estimation error becomes a 50% cost overrun after 100 sessions.
v
How much code you generate per session. Higher velocity accelerates compounding. Control through session scope discipline.
EPM(t)
What energy will cost. Local energy markets determine long-term compute prices. IEA baseline: 1.6%/yr. Hedge scenario: 2.5%/yr. This is the variable that flips your model selection over time.

The Model

One equation. Every cost driver.
Any scenario. Your numbers.

Every rule derives from this equation. Every term was measured empirically – no parameter is theoretical.

C₀ = baseline cost · P = pass rate · ε = Explanation Tax
β = context inflation · v = velocity · N = sessions · A = architecture multiplier

Per session: c(t) = C₀ × EPM(t) × (1 + β·v·t)
Cumulative: C(N,v) = C₀ × EPM(N) × [N + β·v·N(N−1)/2]
Per outcome: CostPerOutcome(K,v) = C(K/P, v) / K
Monthly: Cmo = E × C₀ × EPM(N) × [N + β·v·N(N−1)/2]

Gap: Gap = (A × Verbosity × (1+ε)) × EPM(N)

EPM(t) is an IEA baseline assumption (1.6%/yr). See the chart toggle for aggressive (2.5%) and optimistic (0.8%) scenarios.

Decision Framework

When to move fast.
When to model thoroughly.

Two-Way Doors

Decisions affecting C₀ alone. Reversible. Switch back anytime. Preserves your runway.

  • Switching models per task type
  • Toggling Silent Inference mode
  • Adjusting token limits or caching

Experiment. Measure. Iterate.

One-Way Doors

Decisions affecting β, v, or EPM. Compound through N².

  • Selecting MoE vs dense architecture
  • Multi-year reserved instances
  • Building proprietary agentic frameworks

Model cumulative cost. Project 36 months.

Routing architecture appears two-way but becomes one-way at scale – your distribution locks you into provider model availability.

30-day implementation: Week 1: Classify tasks by complexity. Week 2: Default-route constrained tasks to silent models. Week 3: Establish verification gates and escalation paths. Week 4: Instrument cost metrics. Use CostPerOutcome as your primary KPI.

The Discovery

Both models implement every constraint.
Same output. Different cost structures.

We gave eight models the identical task. Scanned every generated file. DeepSeek and Claude implemented all seven constraints in working Python. The difference: DeepSeek uses GRPO to compress reasoning into latent space. Claude externalizes every reasoning step into billable tokens. DeepSeek is the anchor case study, not an endorsement. The framework measures cost; you decide which model fits your context.
