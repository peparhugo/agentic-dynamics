The Eleven Rules

Here is how you govern
AI costs — and scale your business.

Rules 1–5 are empirically grounded in 248 instrumented experiment sessions. Rules 6–11 extend the framework to autonomous workloads and business outcomes.

Who this is for: Engineering teams of 5+ running Python/Flask workloads with annual AI spend exceeding $100K. Validated on REST APIs, CRUD applications, and real-time collaboration frontends. If your domain differs (TypeScript, Rust, data pipelines, image generation), use the methodology as a guide and validate with your own data.
1
The Silent Inference Rule. Default to the cheapest model that passes your test suite. If a model produces correct code without narration, route to it. Defaulting to silent models preserves your budget runway for the tasks that actually need explicit reasoning – database migrations, core architecture changes, security audits.Concept: Silent Inference Principle · Lever: C₀
2
The Explanation Tax Rule. Narration has a measurable cost. Models with ε > 50% spend 24–120% more per session on output tokens. Whether that cost is worth paying depends on your verification architecture. If automated tests validate output, route to silent models. If you lack test coverage or need auditable reasoning, the Explanation Tax is insurance.Concept: Explanation Tax · Lever: ε
3
The Snowball Rule. Your codebase grows with every session. Each new line of generated code increases the context window for every future session. This isn't additive – it's quadratic. The more you build, the more each subsequent build costs. The β term compounds per-session — model the curve before you commit to an architecture. This is a one-way door.Concept: Snowball Effect (N²) · Lever: β, v · Formula: C(N,v) = C₀ × EPM(N) × [N + β·v·N(N−1)/2]
4
The EPM Horizon Rule. Energy costs are the inflation rate of AI compute. Local energy markets – not global averages – determine your long-term prices. A team in Texas (ERCOT grid) faces different EPM growth than one in Frankfurt (ENTSO-E) or Singapore. Find the year your geographic energy costs flip your model selection. Hedge before that point. EPM(t) = 1.00 + r(t−2024) where r depends on your region.Concept: EPM Horizon · Lever: EPM(t)
5
The Cost-Per-Pass Rule. Price the outcome, not the prompt. A model at half the cost but half the pass rate costs the same per working outcome – with double the latency and retry overhead.Concept: Cost-Per-Pass · Lever: P
6
The Work/Time Tradeoff. Batch processing is 50% cheaper but has a 72-hour horizon. If your task doesn't need a response in <4 hours, run in batch. Monitor queue depth. ExtensionConcept: Batch Economics · Lever: b, Q
7
The Workstream Capacity Constraint. Your maximum throughput is set by your budget, not your infrastructure. Max jobs/day = Budget / (Cost per job × (1 + retry_rate)). Double your budget, double your capacity – zero hiring required. ExtensionConcept: Throughput Cap · Lever: W (jobs/day)
8
The Escalation Cascade. In autonomous workloads, failures auto-escalate through model tiers: Flash → Sonnet → Opus → Human. A 1% escalation to human review can double your total cost. Design for <1% escalation. ExtensionConcept: Auto-Escalation · Lever: Eₔ (28.2× measured: DS → GPT-5.6)
9
The 72-Hour Horizon. Actual batch completion time = (Queue_length × Avg_job_time) + (Retry_buffer × retry_rate). Never batch jobs with an SLA less than 2× your observed queue depth. Always have an on-demand fallback. ExtensionConcept: Batch SLA · Lever: Slack Buffer
10
The WOC Ratio. WOC = 1/(1+r) where r is retry rate. With r=0.214: WOC=0.82 (21.4% overall failure rate). Above 0.85: healthy. Below 0.70: critical – fix immediately. Track WOC by task type, model, and time of day. ExtensionConcept: Efficiency Metric · Lever: WOC Ratio
11
The Outcome Multiplier. The goal isn't to minimize cost – it's to maximize outcomes per dollar. Business Value Index = Total Successful Outcomes / Total AI + Human Cost. A 2× BVI means twice the work for the same cost. A 10× BVI unlocks new business models. ExtensionConcept: Business Value · Lever: BVI
The Levers

What you control. What compounds.
What you must model.

Every variable in the cost equation was measured empirically. Understanding which levers you control — and which compound beyond your control — is the difference between deterministic budgeting and surprise overruns.

Augmented Workforce (Rules 1–5)

These drive the human+AI cost model. Teams using AI coding assistants interact with these every session.

C₀
Which model you call. $0.005 to $1.01 per session. Your primary cost lever. Two-way door – switch per task type.
P
Whether it produces working code. DeepSeek: 72% general pass rate with correct env. Pin frameworks to maximize this. Drives your retry overhead.
ε
How much it narrates. Explanation Tax ranges from 3% (DeepSeek) to 120% (nano). Pay only when you need auditable reasoning.
β
Context inflation per line. Compounds quadratically through the Snowball Rule (N²). Default 0.001 — calibrate to your codebase growth rate.
v
Code velocity per session. How much code you generate per session. Higher velocity accelerates the N² compounding.
EPM(t)
Energy price trajectory. Local energy markets determine long-term compute prices. IEA baseline: 1.6%/yr. Hedge scenario: 2.5%/yr.

Autonomous Workloads (Rules 6–10)

These drive the unsupervised batch-processing cost model. Relevant when AI operates without human-in-the-loop.

C₀
Baseline cost per job. Same model cost lever as augmented, applied to a single autonomous unit of work. $0.001 to $1.01 per job depending on model tier.
b
Batch fraction. What % of jobs run in batch mode. Batch gives 50% discount: D(b) = 1 − b×0.5. Trade-off: longer completion time.
r
Retry rate. Fraction of jobs that fail first-pass and escalate. Measured average: 21.4% across 248 experiments. Drives the retry penalty R(r, Eₔ).
Eₔ
Escalation cost multiplier. How many × more expensive the escalation tier is vs the base model. Measured: 28.2× (DeepSeek → GPT-5.6), 65.7× (DS → Claude). Design for <1% escalation to human.
W
Workload volume. Jobs per day processed autonomously. Throughput limit: Tmax = Budget / Cjob. Double the budget, double the capacity.
EPM(t)
Energy price trajectory. Same multiplier as augmented. Applies to both the base job cost and the retry/escallation overhead.

Decision Framework

When to move fast.
When to model thoroughly.

Two-Way Doors

Decisions affecting C₀ alone. Reversible. Switch back anytime. Preserves your runway.

  • Switching models per task type
  • Toggling Silent Inference mode
  • Adjusting token limits or caching

Experiment. Measure. Iterate.

One-Way Doors

Decisions affecting β, v, or EPM. Compound through N².

  • Selecting MoE vs dense architecture
  • Multi-year reserved instances
  • Building proprietary agentic frameworks

Model cumulative cost. Project 36 months.

Autonomous Doors

Decisions affecting batch routing, escalation chains, and SLA windows. Commit your autonomous workforce architecture for years.

  • Batch vs on-demand workstream split
  • Escalation cascade design (which model when)
  • WOC ratio targets and retry budgets

Design for <1% human escalation. Measure WOC daily.

Routing architecture appears two-way but becomes one-way at scale – your distribution locks you into provider model availability.

30-day implementation: Week 1: Classify tasks by complexity. Week 2: Default-route constrained tasks to silent models. Week 3: Establish verification gates and escalation paths. Week 4: Instrument cost metrics. Use CostPerOutcome as your primary KPI.
